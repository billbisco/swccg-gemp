Manual

Gemp-Swccg is an automatic game client for the Star Wars Customizable Card Game. Below you will find basic information about using it.

  1. The Game Hall
    A place where everything starts. When you log in, you automatically enter the Game Hall. The same thing happens when you win or lose a game. Here you can communicate with other players, create decks, create and join games, and so on.
  2. Building the Deck
    Making a deck is very easy. At the bottom of the Game Hall you will find the "Deck-builder" button. Simply click it and the deck builder will open. At the very top of the screen you will find buttons and other controls that allow you to manage your deck(s). You can create a new deck, save or rename the deck you are editing, or open an existing deck.
    To the right side you can find cards which could be added to your deck. To the left side is what your deck actually consists of.
    The right hand side allows you to filter the available cards by various criteria - side, name, type, etc. You can select multiple criteria and all cards matching these will be displayed below. If there are more cards matching than can be displayed at one time, a next (and previous) page button above will allow you to browse other pages. Between the next and previous button you will find a slider that allows you to adjust the number of cards displayed on each page.
    The displayed card size makes it very difficult to read the card text, therefore you can zoom in on the card by pressing Shift+click on the card. The same option is available without any keyboard interaction by dragging the card in the "up" direction (press the left mouse button on the card, then without releasing the mouse button move your mouse up/forward, then release the mouse button). Once you're done viewing the zoomed card, just click anywhere on the screen.
    Once you find a card you wish to add to your deck, just left-click it to move the card to your deck. If you wish to remove a card from your deck, just left-click it.
    Underneath the deck you will find information about the deck you're building/viewing - the number of cards and whether the deck is valid in the supported formats. If the deck is not valid in some format, the information why it's not valid is available.
  3. Setting up a game
    Once you have a deck, click Play (next to the Deck-builder button at the bottom of the Game Hall) and choose how to play: Play Against Bots, Open Casual Table (a game against another player in the format of your choice), Open League Table or Create Tournament. Pick one of your decks, or a ready-made deck from the deck library, set the format, the game timer and any other options, and click Create Table.
    Your table waits under Waiting Tables until another player joins it with its Join table button (they pick their deck the same way); then the game starts. A casual table can be invite-only (for one named player) or private (nobody can spectate).
    Games in progress are listed under Playing Tables: Play Match takes you back to your own game, and Spectate lets you watch anyone else's game that is not private.
  4. Playing the game
    Playing the Star Wars Customizable Card Game via Gemp is similar to playing it in real life. In most cases cards eligible to play at the current moment will be highlighted. If you don't want to do anything, click Pass and the next step will commence.
    The Settings tab allows you to change some basic settings of the game - whether to automatically pass if no phase actions are available to you or not, and also whether each card/action selection has to be confirmed or not. It's highly recommended to de-select this checkbox if you're playing on touch-screen devices to prevent accidental card selection.
    The displayed card size makes it very difficult to read the card text, therefore you can zoom in on the card by pressing Shift+click on the card. During a game this also displays the card's live stats (including modifiers). The same option is available without any keyboard interaction by dragging the card in the "up" direction (press the left mouse button on the card, then without releasing the mouse button move your mouse up/forward, then release the mouse button). Once you're done viewing the zoomed card, just click anywhere on the screen.
  5. Player stats
    During a game you can see each player's name at the top-left of the window. To the right of the name you can find the "chess-clock" for that player. This measures the time it took that player to make decisions in the game (like in chess). If the time for a player runs out, that player automatically concedes the game (loses). Directly below the player name you will see an icon with a number: the hand size. Next to it you will see an extra button with a plus (+) sign, that allows you to get extra data about that player's Force generation and cards out of play.
    You can browse your own or your opponent's out-of-play cards by clicking on the icon.
  6. The Iron Rule
    There is no rolling back, simple as that. You can't undo an action that already happened, even if your opponent agrees. If you played cards you didn't want to, performed the wrong action, or didn't trigger an optional action, it's done and can't be reversed. If you make mistakes like that often, you might consider unchecking the "Auto-accept after selecting action or card" checkbox in the Settings tab.
  7. Useful Stuff
    Across the top of the Game Hall you will find tabs with useful information: the format definitions and league rules under Help, the running leagues and tournaments under Events, and your own games and statistics under My Account.
